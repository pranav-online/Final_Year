# Rebuild ALIP broker demand analysis on real mandi data

Rebuild ALIP's broker Demand Analysis as a **price-and-arrivals intelligence pipeline**. Public mandi data feeds it. Its descriptive signals are computed from that data, its forecasts are checked against baselines, and the broker sees decisions such as where to sell, when to act, which farmer listings to buy and how urgent the situation is. Today the feature is a regressor trained on **56 hand-typed rows** with no time dimension, no real prices and no evaluation, and nothing tells the user that the numbers are invented. Most of what the master report describes for `/demand` does not exist in the code: the Z-score detector, the urgency scorer, the 7-day price decay, the seasonal context, the 7-factor ranker and shipment initiation. The fix starts by changing what "demand" means. Agmarknet-derived data records prices and arrivals but not consumption, so the system should report an **inferred demand–supply pressure** together with 1–4 week **price forecasts with 80% ranges**. It should not report a kilogram "expected demand" figure. The data backbone is three sources: the official data.gov.in daily mandi price API (current day only, so it must be pulled every day), a one-time CEDA (Ashoka University) backfill for history, and the open Agmarknet 2.0 endpoints for national arrivals and weekly trends. The farmer listings already in the app become ALIP's distinctive forward-supply signal, which no public source has. "Real time" for a broker means a few refreshes a day, because the mandi feeds update daily. Instant matching on new farmer listings and alerts is where the real-time value lies. The work splits into six phases (0–5). Each phase has acceptance checks the team can run, and the master report should be revised at the end so it describes the system that actually runs.

## The master report describes five engines; the code ships one synthetic regressor

The master report's §2.5 "Predictive Demand & Anomaly Analytics" takes **Region + Crop + Supply Volume (kg)** as input and feeds it to five analyzers. The first is a dynamic absorption threshold, `Threshold = Base × M_region_type × M_density × M_market_size`. The second is a supply Z-score, `Z = (supply − μ)/σ`, with bands at 1 and 2. The third is a 1–10 urgency score weighted by perishability tier (Tomato and Banana ×2.0, Potato, Onion and Maize ×1.3, grains ×1.0). The fourth is a 7-day price decay, `P(t) = P0(1−δ)^t`, with a break-even day drawn on a Recharts AreaChart. The fifth is a seasonal harvest context. The report also promises a 7-factor destination ranker over a "45+ Mandi Spatial Distance Index", and a shipment lifecycle that starts when the broker "confirms deal in Demand Analysis" ([master report §2.2–2.10](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Downloads/alip_farmer_broker_master_report2.md)). It never names a market-price data source. Its only evaluation metrics (NDCG@3 = 0.9128 on a 2014 holdout) belong to crop recommendation, not demand. The report has no "changes needed" section. Everything in it is written as if already built ([master report §1.3, §7](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Downloads/alip_farmer_broker_master_report2.md)).

The running code does far less. The React page takes a single region string and calls `GET /market/demand/{region}` ([DemandAnalysis.js](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Final_Year/frontend/alip-frontend/src/pages/DemandAnalysis.js)). The backend first sums the quantities of every "available" farmer listing for that region, whenever each was posted. If there are none, it **silently substitutes a hard-coded 7-city demo seed**. In the Mongo path it goes further: a region with no supply gets Hyderabad's demo data back under the requested region's name ([market_service.py](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Final_Year/backend/services/market_service.py)). For each crop it then asks a `GradientBoostingRegressor` for "expected demand" in about 27 candidate regions. That regressor was trained on 56 invented rows covering 7 crops, has no zaid season and no cited source. Its six features are crop code, season, region type, population density, market size and month, so its output is effectively a smoothed lookup over static city attributes ([demand_ml_service.py](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Final_Year/backend/services/demand_ml_service.py)). Tomato and onion are not in its crop map. These are the crops behind the report's urgency tiers and its flagship Guntur example, and they fall back to static thresholds. The status rules are oversupply above 1.5× threshold and undersupply below 0.5×. The report's alert rule is "exceed capacity by > 15%". Price impact comes from a constant elasticity table, for example rice at ₹22 and −0.35 ([demand_analysis.py](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Final_Year/backend/services/demand_analysis.py)).

The lesson for the redesign is that the report's vocabulary is sound and its data is fictional. Every one of its five engines needs **dated observations** of supply and price. Neither the listings collection nor any other store in the project holds such observations. Adding a time dimension backed by real data is the core of the redesign. Everything else depends on it.

## Demand is unobservable, so measure pressure and forecast price instead

Agmarknet reports a daily min, max and modal price and an arrival quantity per market and commodity. It records no offtake, so true demand cannot be observed. Studies of Maharashtra onion markets find a negative relationship between arrivals and price across years. In some months, though, arrivals and price rise together, which is what happens when a demand shift outweighs a supply shift ([IJH](https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/214/1434)). An RBI working paper found a **significant negative relation between monthly availability and tomato, onion and potato prices**, and found that SARIMAX models with supply "balance-sheet" variables forecast better ([RBI WPS 8/2024](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/30842423/WPS+No.+8%2C+2024+-+Vegetables+Inflation+in+India.pdf)). Taken together, these support the honest framing: arrivals are realised supply, price is the clearing outcome, and demand pressure is inferred from how price moves relative to arrivals compared with the seasonal norm.

The redesign therefore drops the "expected demand (kg)" number and replaces it with a **Demand–Supply Pressure Index (DSPI)**. The DSPI combines an arrivals ratio (7-day arrivals over the median for the same ISO week in the previous three years), a price ratio against the same seasonal norm, and 4- and 13-week momentum. Its output is four quadrants a broker can act on. High arrivals with a rising price means strong demand. Low arrivals with a rising price means a supply shortage. High arrivals with a falling price means a glut. Low arrivals with a falling price means weak demand. No Indian study formally defines a composite mandi demand index of this kind, so the DSPI is a design proposal and should be presented as one. The UI must label it "inferred pressure", never "demand". Elasticity is not constant. An IIM Bangalore analysis reportedly found a mandi demand curve that is flat below a threshold price and has an elasticity of about −1 above it ([IIMB](https://iimb.ac.in/node/3547), summary-level). That is why the price–arrival relationship should be fitted in log space or learned by a tree model rather than read from the current fixed elasticity table.

Broker practice shows which outputs matter. Arhtiyas sort, weigh and sell produce, route payments and earn **2–4% commission**. They are also lenders whose credit ties farmers to them ([Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/agriculture/addressing-the-economic-trade-offs-of-interlinkages-in-contemporary-agrarian-markets); [PIDE](https://pide.org.pk/research/the-role-of-arthi-in-agriculture-marketing-an-exploiter-or-facilitator-of-farmers/)). Their worst losses come from glut crashes in perishables. Chhindwara tomatoes fell from more than ₹200/kg to about ₹2/kg in January 2025 ([ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/en/!state/tomato-and-cauliflower-prices-crash-in-madhya-pradesh-enn25010402109)). Madhya Pradesh onions sold at or below ₹1/kg in June 2025 against a production cost of ₹12–15/kg ([Global Agriculture](https://www.global-agriculture.com/india-region/onion-mandi-prices-crash-in-madhya-pradesh-farmers-unable-to-recover-even-transportation-costs/)). Indian markets are also imperfectly integrated, and arbitrage gains persist even after transport costs ([World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/60fe08d8-4be7-5db5-9c94-fdb7a2429556/full)). The useful outputs are therefore early glut warnings, cross-mandi spreads net of freight, and a view of incoming supply. Mandi-bhav apps already offer daily prices, multi-mandi comparison and alerts. Arrival-surge alerts, net-of-transport ranking, forecasts with uncertainty bands and listing-linked supply are rarely offered to small brokers ([acrop.app](https://acrop.app/mandi); [Decentro](https://decentro.tech/blog/agritech-startups/)). Those are the features ALIP should build to stand apart.

The report's five engines map cleanly onto data-grounded replacements, which lets the team keep the report's structure and still be honest about how each part works:

| Report engine | Redesigned implementation | Data it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamic absorption threshold | **Seasonal arrival norm**: median arrivals for the same ISO week over the previous 3 years, per market or state and commodity. "Oversupply" = arrivals ratio > 1.15, matching the report's >15% rule | Arrivals history (CEDA or Agmarknet), falling back to the price-only norm |
| Z-score supply anomaly | **Robust z-score** (median/MAD) of weekly price change and of arrivals against the seasonal norm; \|z\| ≥ 2 elevated, ≥ 3 critical | Same history |
| 1–10 urgency scorer | Keep the report's formula and tiers. Feed it the real arrivals ratio, the forecast downside (P10) and the listing age | Signals, forecasts, listings |
| 7-day price decay and break-even | Replace the assumed δ with the **forecast fan** (P10/P50/P90 at 1, 2 and 4 weeks). Break-even day = first horizon at which P50 at the destination minus freight falls below P50 at the source | Forecasts, freight table |
| Seasonal harvest context | **Seasonal index** from history (month or week of year), plus a flag for whether the arrivals peak is rising or receding | Price and arrivals history |
| 7-factor ranker | **Net realisation ranking**: forecast P50 at arrival minus freight minus commission and fees, with P10 downside. Reliability and storage factors stay switched off until shipment and rating data exist | Forecasts, mandi coordinates, broker settings |

## A daily-ingest pipeline feeds signals, forecasts and decisions through one FastAPI service

### Three data sources, each with a defined job

The **data.gov.in OGD resource `9ef84268-d588-465a-a308-a864a43d0070`** ("Current Daily Price of Various Commodities from Various Markets (Mandi)") is the only official, documented, key-based mandi price API. It was verified live on 2026-10-14/15 at **19,095 records nationally**. Its fields are state, district, market, commodity, variety, grade, a `dd/mm/yyyy` arrival_date string, and min, max and modal price in **₹/quintal**. It has no arrivals. It serves **only the current day** ([api.data.gov.in](https://api.data.gov.in/resource/9ef84268-d588-465a-a308-a864a43d0070)). The public sample key returns at most 10 rows per request even when `limit=1000` is set, so the team must register a personal key and page through results with `offset` until `offset ≥ total`. A working open-source collector shows the trap: it trusted `limit` and stopped after the first 10-row page. The same collector found that the feed fills during the day and should be pulled after about 14:00 IST ([agri-commodity-tracker](https://github.com/herrrickshaw/agri-commodity-tracker)). A missed day is lost from this source. Market names carry trailing spaces (`"APMC Nasik "`), so normalisation is mandatory. A third party describes the feed as "frequently down / WAF-blocked" ([agmarket-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Krishna-Baldwa/agmarket-mcp)). The catalogue page has also shown a "Request API" note that contradicts the live test ([data.gov.in](https://data.gov.in/resource/current-daily-price-various-commodities-various-markets-mandi)). Retries and a health endpoint are therefore part of the design, not optional extras.

**Agmarknet 2.0** relaunched in November 2025 as a React app backed by `https://api.agmarknet.gov.in/v1/`. It covers about 4,367 linked mandis ([VisionIAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/schemes/ministry-of-agriculture-farmers-welfare/integrated-scheme-for-agricultural-marketing-schemes-agmarknet-portal)). Several endpoints answered anonymous requests in testing. `/commodities?page_size=500` returns **605 commodities** with IDs, for example Onion = 23, Potato = 24 and Tomato = 65. `/location/state` returns states and districts with **LGD codes**. `POST /dashboard-data/` with `{"dashboard":"marketwise_price_arrival", ..., "downloadreport":"false"}` returns national commodity prices and **arrivals in metric tonnes** for today and the two previous days. `/price-trend/wholesale-prices-weekly` returns state-wise weekly prices ([api.agmarknet.gov.in](https://api.agmarknet.gov.in/v1/commodities?page_size=3)). The market-level daily price and arrival report is **behind a captcha** (`TOKEN_OR_CAPTCHA_REQUIRED`). These endpoints are undocumented internals with no published terms. ALIP should use them for **reference masters and national arrival context only**, and must not scrape the captcha-protected report. The old `Default.aspx` scrapers target the retired site and should not be copied.

The **CEDA Agri Market API** (`https://api.ceda.ashoka.edu.in/v1`, Bearer key) mirrors Agmarknet from **2000 to the present with a lag of a few months**. Prices are queried by commodity, state and date range, and the API returns HTTP 429 when rate-limited ([agmarket-mcp api.py](https://github.com/Krishna-Baldwa/agmarket-mcp/blob/feat/ceda-api-migration/src/agmarknet_mcp/api.py)). An unauthenticated call was verified to return "Unauthorised, no api key passed" ([CEDA API](https://api.ceda.ashoka.edu.in/v1/agmarknet/commodities)). CEDA provides the one-time historical backfill. Its README describes a cleaned price *and arrival* dataset ([CEDA README](https://agmarknet.ceda.ashoka.edu.in/README.html)). The `markets` endpoint takes an `indicator` parameter, which suggests arrivals are queryable, but this was not verified. **Market-level arrivals are the project's largest data risk.** The team should confirm CEDA's arrival indicator in Phase 1. If it is unavailable, the DSPI should fall back to price-only signals plus national arrivals from Agmarknet and listing-based supply. CEDA uses census IDs and Agmarknet uses LGD codes, so a mapping table is required. As an offline test fixture, a Mendeley dataset combines Agmarknet onion prices and arrivals with IMD weather for 2014–2024 ([Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/ds9jmxp9zy)). eNAM has no public API and should be left out ([eNAM dashboard](https://enam.gov.in/web/dashboard/trade-data)).

The project should **limit scope deliberately**. It should cover about 8–10 commodities that match the app's crops and the report's tiers: Tomato, Onion, Potato, Banana, Maize, Paddy/Rice, Wheat, Cotton and Groundnut. It should cover the states behind its regions: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. The backfill window should be about 2018 to the present, chunked by year. That keeps CEDA calls, storage and training time manageable on a laptop.

### Storage, jobs and the JSON fallback

The backend should keep its single FastAPI process. It should put a **repository interface** in front of storage, with a `MongoRepo` and a `JsonFileRepo` behind it. This generalises the project's existing pattern of catching `PyMongoError` and falling back to the local JSON listings store into one interface ([local_market_store.py](file:///C:/Users/rahul/Final_Year/backend/services/local_market_store.py)). The collections are `mandi_prices_raw`, `price_observations`, `market_arrivals`, `daily_market_stats`, `forecasts`, `alerts`, `model_registry`, `markets`, `commodities` and `broker_settings`, plus the existing `market_data` listings with added fields. `mandi_prices_raw` keeps every pulled row keyed on its natural key (date, state, district, market, commodity, variety, grade, source), and `price_observations` holds the cleaned series. `price_observations` can be a MongoDB time-series collection with `timeField: "date"`, a stable `metaField` of {commodity, state, district, market}, and `granularity: "hours"` for daily data. The metaField cannot be redefined after creation, and high-cardinality meta values create sparse buckets ([MongoDB considerations](https://mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/timeseries/timeseries-considerations)). Time-series collections are a poor fit for unique-key upserts, so the simpler choice for a student team is a **normal collection with a compound unique index** on the natural key. The scheduler is **APScheduler 3.x `AsyncIOScheduler`**, started in the FastAPI lifespan and guarded by `SCHEDULER_ENABLED`, with `max_instances=1` and `coalesce=True`. Every uvicorn worker would otherwise fire each job, and 4.x changes the API ([fixdevs](https://fixdevs.com/blog/apscheduler-not-working/)).

| Job (IST) | Action |
|---|---|
| `ingest_prices` at 15:00 and 23:30, plus a 06:00 catch-up | Page through data.gov.in, normalise, validate and upsert; write an `ingest_reports` row |
| `ingest_arrivals` daily | Agmarknet `dashboard-data` national arrivals, best-effort |
| `compute_features` after each ingest | Weekly aggregates, seasonal norms, robust z-scores, momentum, volatility, DSPI |
| `run_alerts` after features | Glut, spike and crash alerts, listing matches; deduplicated per 24 h |
| `forecast_nightly` at 23:45 | Predict P10/P50/P90 at h = 1, 2 and 4 weeks for every active series; precompute best-market tables |
| `retrain_weekly` on Sunday at 02:00 | Rolling-origin backtest; promote only if the gate passes |

The FastAPI layer serves only from storage, never from live upstream calls, so a government outage cannot break the broker UI.

### Forecasting: baselines first, then one global gradient-boosted model

Indian studies cover ARIMA, hybrids, tree ensembles and recurrent networks. Reported errors on Agmarknet vegetables cluster in the **low teens to mid-20s % MAPE**: a GRU reached about 14.6% for onion and 10.6% for tomato on 2010–2024 data across 165 markets ([Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12215695/)), and ARIMA-only onion studies in Gujarat reached 22–28% ([DSIMS](https://dsims.org.in/assets/files/r-vol/1.%20Mr.%20Parth%20Laijawala%20&%20Mr.%20Maneesh%20Gupta_1-19.pdf)). None of the retrieved studies tested against a seasonal-naive baseline, and many "deep beats ARIMA" results compare against a weak ARIMA. A student project earns credibility by including the baselines that papers often skip. In the M5 competition, **global LightGBM models trained across many related series won**, and machine learning clearly beat statistical benchmarks there for the first time ([M5 results](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/M5_accuracy_competition.pdf)).

ALIP should forecast **weekly median modal price** for each (commodity, market). Daily series have holiday and reporting gaps. It should use three direct horizons (h = 1, 2 and 4 weeks) and three quantiles (0.1, 0.5 and 0.9), which makes nine small models. The target is the log of the price ratio to the current week. Features are price lags (1–52 weeks), shifted rolling statistics, the min–max spread, arrivals anomaly where available, week-of-year as sin/cos, a crop-season flag, festival windows from the `holidays` package, the lagged state median and lead-market spread (Lasalgaon for onion, Azadpur for vegetables), and categorical IDs. Every rolling feature must use `groupby(series).shift(1)` before rolling, to prevent leakage. Seasonal norms come from prior years only. The baselines, built first, are naive, seasonal naive and a 4-week moving average, with optional per-series SARIMA for the top 10–20 series. The library choice is one of two. LightGBM is what the master report already claims, but it must be added to requirements. scikit-learn's `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` with quantile loss needs no new dependency. Prophet and LSTM/GRU are stretch goals at most. Series with fewer than 26 weeks of history should use a hierarchical fallback: the state median price times the series' historical ratio to the state, flagged "low confidence".

Evaluation should follow rolling-origin practice. Every training window ends before its test point, and error is reported at the horizon that matters ([FPP3 §5.10](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)). The protocol holds out the last 52 weeks, takes expanding-window origins every 4 weeks over the prior 2–3 years, and reports MAE in ₹/quintal, RMSE, **MASE against seasonal naive** (below 1 means the model beats the baseline), MAPE for communication, and directional accuracy. FPP3 prefers MASE because MAPE misbehaves near zero ([FPP3 §5.8](https://otexts.com/fpp3/accuracy.html)). Intervals come from quantile models or split-conformal residuals. **Coverage must be checked empirically**: in-sample residuals delivered only about 61% coverage for a nominal 80% interval in skforecast's documentation ([skforecast](https://skforecast.org/latest/user_guides/probabilistic-forecasting-conformal-prediction)). A model is promoted only if MASE < 1 on most series and 80% coverage falls between 70% and 90%. Any series where the model loses to seasonal naive shows **"Not enough signal"** instead of a forecast. That rule is the clearest single fix for the current design, which presents invented numbers as fact.

### API surface

The new endpoints stay under the report's `/market/demand/*` namespace so the report's §7 catalogue can be corrected rather than rewritten. All of them require a validated JWT with `role == "broker"` through a FastAPI dependency, which fixes the current client-only role guard. Fixed-path routes must be registered before the legacy `/market/demand/{region}` path parameter, or FastAPI will match `overview` as a region name.

| Endpoint | Returns |
|---|---|
| `GET /market/demand/overview?state=&commodities=` | Heatmap rows: commodity × market with DSPI quadrant, arrivals ratio, price vs norm, robust z, momentum, freshness |
| `GET /market/demand/analysis/{commodity}?market=&qty_kg=` | The report's "full analysis": signal card (pressure, urgency 1–10, seasonal context), forecast fan, break-even day, top-5 net-realisation destinations, matched listings |
| `GET /market/prices/history?commodity=&market=&weeks=52` | Weekly min, modal and max, plus arrivals where known |
| `GET /market/demand/forecast/{commodity}/{market}` | P10/P50/P90 for h = 1, 2 and 4 weeks, model_version, backtest MASE, coverage, confidence chip |
| `GET /market/demand/best-markets?commodity=&from=&radius_km=500&qty_kg=&truck=` | Ranked destinations by net ₹/quintal, using the report's truck rates (₹25/35/50 per km) as editable defaults; includes ROI |
| `GET /market/demand/matches` | Farmer listings ranked by pressure, distance, quantity fit and expected margin |
| `GET /market/demand/alerts?since=` and `POST .../alerts/{id}/read` | Glut, spike, crash, target-price and listing-match alerts |
| `POST /market/demand/initiate-shipment` | Creates a `shipments` document (state `initiated`), as the report specifies |
| `GET /health/data` | Last successful pull per source, row counts, unmapped-name rate, stale markets |

Responses carry `is_demo`, `data_as_of` and `source` on every payload. An in-process TTL cache, cleared after each ingest, keeps cached endpoints fast.

### UI

The page should be rebuilt as a decision screen, not a chart gallery. The top of the page holds **action cards**, one per watched commodity. Each card shows SELL, HOLD or WATCH with a plain reason, for example "Arrivals 2.1× the seasonal norm; price likely to fall 8–15% over 2 weeks", and a High, Medium or Low confidence chip derived from backtest MASE and interval width. Below the cards sit a commodity × market pressure heatmap, a **fan chart**, a best-market table, matched listings and an alerts feed. The fan chart uses a Recharts `ComposedChart` with a P10–P90 `Area`, a P50 `Line`, a "today" `ReferenceLine`, and the report's break-even line. Recharts 3.8.1 is already installed. Fan charts were the uncertainty format that a 2026 Bank of England study's participants understood most readily, and they reduced loss of trust after forecast misses ([Bank of England WP 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)). An eye-tracking study found that adding a median line makes such charts easier to read ([Geoscience Communication 2023](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/6/111/2023/gc-6-111-2023.pdf)). Ranges should be worded as "8 in 10 times the price lands between ₹1,800 and ₹2,300 per quintal", not "80% PI". Every view carries a **data-freshness badge** ("Prices as of 14 Oct 23:30, 3 markets not reported"), because Agmarknet-derived feeds can lag a day ([acrop.app](https://acrop.app/mandi)). The broker dashboard's invented "Active Deals" figure should be replaced with real counts of oversupply and undersupply alerts and open listings.

## The outcome: a broker morning briefing that updates when farmers list

The outcome is not one prediction. It is a short list of decisions, each with a price range and a confidence level the broker can check. Take the report's own worked example. A farmer lists **2,500 kg of tomato in Guntur at ₹22/kg**. In the redesigned system the listing is geocoded to the nearest known mandi and enters the listing-supply aggregate straight away. A match job then sends a `listing_match` alert to brokers whose watchlist and radius cover Guntur tomato. When the broker opens `/demand`, the analysis for tomato at Guntur shows the arrivals ratio against the seasonal norm and the robust z-score, so a statement like "Z = 2.4" comes from real data. It shows the fan chart for 1, 2 and 4 weeks. It shows the urgency score, using the report's Tier 1 multiplier of ×2.0 and its 1–2 day action window. It ranks destinations within 500 km by forecast P50 at the arrival date minus freight and commission. A row might read: "Hyderabad, 280 km, medium truck: net ₹X/quintal, P10 downside ₹Y, ROI 2.1×." Clicking dispatch calls `initiate-shipment`, which closes the loop the report describes in §3.2. Every number in that flow can be traced to an observation or a backtested model.

Real-time value has to match the data's real refresh rate. Mandi prices change once a day and some markets report a day late. Polling every 5–15 minutes, plus alerts on ingest and on new listings, is enough, and WebSockets add nothing ([OneUptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-25-build-realtime-dashboards-fastapi/markdown)). The genuinely live elements are ALIP's own: farmer listings arriving during the day, glut alerts firing after the afternoon ingest, and the broker's editable freight and commission settings recomputing net realisation immediately. **Listing-driven supply** is the moat. Agmarknet tells a broker what reached the mandi yesterday. ALIP can tell them what farmers within 100 km intend to sell over the next 7–14 days. Adding that to arrivals gives a "possible glut" flag before the crash shows up in prices, which is the risk that hit the Chhindwara and Madhya Pradesh onion markets. The same signals can flow back to the farmer's crop-recommendation and listing screens as "best place and time to sell" advice, as the report's §3.1 loop promises. They should be shown next to the mandi modal price, for transparency.

The remaining uncertainty is about usefulness, not engineering. No study was found that measures what forecast accuracy Indian brokers consider actionable. No study was found that evaluates a best-market recommender against realised outcomes. No published per-km freight norms exist per commodity, which is why freight must stay an editable parameter. The final evaluation should therefore include a simple **simulated-margin backtest**: run the SELL/HOLD rule over the hold-out year and compare its realised margin with an "always sell today" policy.

The phases are ordered so that each one leaves a working, demonstrable system. Phase 0 is housekeeping. Phases 1–3 build the data and modelling core. Phases 4–5 turn it into broker decisions and a revised report. Effort estimates assume a 3–4 person final-year team.

## Conclusion

The redesign succeeds or fails on whether every number a broker sees can be traced back to a mandi observation or a model that beat a seasonal-naive baseline. Algorithm choice matters much less. That standard makes this a stronger final-year project, not a weaker one. "Our model loses to seasonal naive on 30% of series, and the UI hides forecasts there" is a defensible, examinable result. A synthetic regressor that cannot be evaluated is neither. The master report's ambition can stay. Its engines simply need data underneath them, and its §2.5 needs rewriting to say so.

ALIP's real advantage does not come from forecasting skill, where published Indian results have room for improvement and weak baselines. It comes from joining public arrivals and prices with **its own farmer listings** to see a glut forming before it reaches the mandi. Two things decide how much of that advantage is achievable: whether market-level arrival history can be obtained from CEDA, and whether the team keeps the data.gov.in pull running every single day from now on. Both should be settled in the first two weeks, before any model work begins.
